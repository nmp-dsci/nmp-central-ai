Should MLflow be replaced by Langfuse?

A measured comparison of the two as self-hosted, open-source platforms, scored across 52 features weighted by what this portfolio actually does. Plus the industry-standard question, and the other self-hosted options worth knowing about.

Every number about the current stack was read from the live platform on 2026-10-02 (MLflow 3.16.1, Postgres, MinIO) and from the sibling repos — not estimated. Section 10 lists the commands.

The short answer

Do not swap. Split the job. Scored on the whole feature surface the two land almost level — MLflow 72% vs Langfuse 74% weighted — but that near-tie hides the real shape: they lead in different halves. Langfuse wins GenAI observability 48–26. MLflow wins the run-and-model ledger 40–9. A swap would buy you the first by giving up the second.

The decisive local fact: this portfolio uses MLflow as a ledger far more than as a tracer. There are 9,928 recorded metrics across 835 keys, 743 params and 2,923 report artifacts — and zero of those metrics use a step series. Langfuse has no artifact store and no params table, so a pure swap orphans 81.9 MB of evaluation reports and the sweep-comparison habit built on them.

05  Where the seam would go

One concept: your instrumentation already speaks OTLP in 13 places, and Langfuse ingests OTLP over HTTP. That line is the whole migration for the tracing half.

Why this is cheap. Langfuse accepts OTLP over HTTP at /api/public/otel/v1/traces with Basic auth. Your OTLP projects (P5 data-qa is already pure OTLP) change an endpoint and an auth header. The MLflow-SDK projects keep their @mlflow.trace decorators or switch to an OTel exporter — your call, per project.

One constraint to design around: Langfuse supports OTLP over HTTP only. gRPC is not supported, so any exporter configured for grpc must move to http/protobuf.

06  What is the industry standard?

Two different questions hide in this one, and they have different answers.

The wire format — this is the standard

OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions (gen_ai.*) are what every serious vendor now ingests, MLflow and Langfuse included. Instrument to OTel and the backend becomes a deployment choice rather than a rewrite.

But it is not stable. As of mid-2026 every gen_ai.* attribute, span, metric and event in the registry still carries the Development stability badge — none is marked Stable. In June 2026 (semconv v1.42.0) the GenAI conventions were moved out of the main repository into a dedicated semantic-conventions-genai repo, which has no tagged release yet, so there is no versioned schema URL to pin against.

Practical read: the core shape (operation, provider, model, token usage) has held since v1.37.0. Instrument the core now and expect to add attributes, not rewrite them.

The product — Langfuse leads

Among self-hostable LLM-observability products Langfuse is the clear adoption leader by the crude-but-public measure: 35.3k GitHub stars vs MLflow's 28.2k, with the broadest framework integration surface and an MIT core.

There is no standards body here and no neutral benchmark. "Industry standard" for the platform layer means "most widely adopted", and that is Langfuse — while MLflow remains the standard for the ML lifecycle layer, which is a different job.

Governance note: Langfuse was acquired by ClickHouse in early 2026 — the repository's own LICENSE now reads "Copyright (c) 2023-2026 ClickHouse, Inc." Its database dependency and its owner are now the same company.
