04 — the per-project colour

One number per project, and contrast stops being your problem

The trap: a single accent hex cannot serve both themes

Measured against the light paper background #F7F6F2, every dark-theme accent in your portfolio fails AA — the house teal #5cc8a6 scores 1.89:1, data-qa's amber 2.01:1, transcript-rag's blue 2.15:1. So "project X is teal" cannot be one hex. It has to be a pair, and picking pairs by hand for 9+ projects is how a palette quietly goes inaccessible.

Your own files already point at the fix. Converting the eight accents in use to OKLCH shows you have been unconsciously holding lightness and chroma constant and varying only hue:

06 — your second question

"Would I update it here, then get each project to adopt it?"

Yes — and the reason to be deliberate about how is that "adopt" hides two different wants.

Want 1 — new artifacts get the latest solved by any single source

Every artifact written after the change should look current without anyone remembering to update it. A linked file gives you this for free.

Want 2 — existing artifacts restyle retroactively costs more than it looks

Only a live link (symlink or CDN) does this. But your artifacts are dated receipts — s02_sql-frontend.html documents a trial that happened on 22 Sep. Making 277 of them mutable means a future token rename can silently break a two-year-old page you cite in a PR, and you would not find out.

The loop, concretely

Edit one file here agent/house/house.css — or registry/projects.yaml to change a project's hue
make house-ui re-renders, then fails if any declared hue misses AA on any audited pair in either theme
make install-house-ui copies the canon into each sibling's .lavish/, cmp-guarded, FORCE=1 to overwrite a locally edited copy — exactly how install-agent-context behaves today
New artifacts pick it up automatically they carry <link rel=stylesheet href="house.css"> and one --hue line, so the next artifact in any project is current with no instruction
Existing artifacts stay as written until you deliberately re-link one; `make house-ui-check` tells you which projects are behind

08 — risks and open questions

What could go wrong, and what I could not decide for you

risk oklch() is a 2023 baseline

Deriving accents live in CSS needs oklch() — Safari 15.4+, Chrome 111+, Firefox 113+. Fine today; a 2019 browser would show unstyled accents. Mitigation if that matters: have house_ui.py bake hex per hue instead. D28 chooses.

risk the site is the current owner

tokens.css and contrast_audit.py live in a repo with its own CI. Moving the canon here means the site consumes rather than owns, and its build must not break. Sequence S7 last and keep the site's main.css where it is — those components are site furniture, not artifact furniture.

risk 21 vendored copies drift silently

The one real cost of option B. The version stamp plus house-ui-check in make check is what turns a silent drift into a red row.

open which directories are in scope

Nine projects are in the registry; 21 have artifacts. ai-engineer-fit (65 artifacts) and databricks-* are not platform projects but are your biggest consumers. D26 asks whether the house UI fans out to all of them or only to registered projects.

open hue 168 has four claimants

DABStep, DataAgentBench, vidore and the site all use #43C29A today. The table above gives the house hue to the platform and the site and moves the three projects. If you would rather DAB keep the green, say so and I will re-solve the wheel around it.

open Geist vs IBM Plex

This repo's three artifacts are the only Geist users; 12 directories are on IBM Plex, and the self-hosted woff2 already exist. The plan retires Geist. It is a real aesthetic choice, not just a count — D27.
